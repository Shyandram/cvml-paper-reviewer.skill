# Review: Falcon: A Cross-Modal Evaluation Dataset for Comprehensive Safety Perception

(Based on extracted text only. Figures were not available, so Figs. 4–6 are judged from captions and text.)

## Summary
The paper presents Falcon, a set of 57,515 vision-language QA triples (image, instruction, response) labeled for harmfulness across 13 categories. Prompts and images come from SPA-VL, JailBreakV-28K and HADES. Responses were generated by three open 7B MLLMs (DeepSeek-VL-7B-Base, MiniCPM-V-2.6, Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Labels (instruction, image and response harmfulness, categories, and an explanation) were produced by Qwen2.5-VL-72B-AWQ. A 1,800-sample test subset, Falcon-test, was labeled by humans. The authors LoRA-fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the training split to get FalconEye, a harm evaluator. They report accuracy gains over Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Beaver-dam, Llama-Guard-3 and GPT-4o on Falcon-test, VLGuard (image and instruction) and BeaverTails-V (response).

## Strengths
- The problem is relevant. A cheap open evaluator for multimodal safety that judges the image, instruction and response separately is useful, and per-modality labels are a sensible design.
- The dataset is large. Responses come from several models, and the data includes benign samples. The CC BY 4.0 license and open model release help reproducibility.
- There is a human-labeled test set, plus out-of-distribution evaluation on VLGuard and BeaverTails-V. The appendix honestly reports where FalconEye loses to GPT-4o (category detection, Table 7).
- The annotation/training prompt, the attack prompt and the category definitions are given.

## Weaknesses
1. **Evaluation protocol and baseline fairness.**
   - Accuracy is the only metric, on heavily imbalanced data: only 19.2% of instructions are safe, while 80.6% of images are safe (Fig. 4a). Accuracy is uninformative without a majority-class baseline. Precision, recall and F1 for the harmful class, AUROC and confusion matrices are needed.
   - No inter-annotator agreement is reported for Falcon-test. The number of annotators, the adjudication procedure and the guidelines are not given. Section 5 itself admits "inconsistencies in labeling".
   - Baselines are not given the same task or taxonomy. The prompts for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and GPT-4o are unspecified. Llama-Guard-3-11B-Vision, the most direct competitor named in the Introduction, appears only on BeaverTails-V and is missing from Tables 2–4.
   - GPT-4o refused 25 samples, which were dropped from Table 3. Those are probably the hardest, most harmful cases. Refusals should count as errors or be reported separately.
   - No confidence intervals or seeds. The gains over GPT-4o are small (94.31 vs 93.13 on response, 90.93 vs 88.56 on instruction, n = 1,775) and may be within noise.
2. **Train/test circularity and label provenance.**
   - FalconEye is trained on labels from Qwen2.5-VL-72B, the same family as its base model, so it inherits the teacher's errors and biases. Falcon-test shares source datasets, prompt styles and response-generating models with the training data. The in-distribution gain over base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (e.g., 76→91 on instruction) mostly shows that task-specific fine-tuning helps. It does not show "robust generalization".
   - Agreement between the 72B labels and the human labels is not reported. Without it, "effectively serving the role of a human auditor" is unsupported, and training label noise is unknown.
   - Near-duplicate leakage between train and test is not addressed. JailBreakV-28K in particular has many templated variants.
3. **Out-of-distribution claims are weak and partly contradictory.**
   - On BeaverTails-V, the overall gain is 77.12 vs 75.09 for Llama-Guard-3-8B, a text-only model that does not see the image. That weakens the "visual information matters" thesis.
   - Table 6 shows FalconEye far below baselines in many categories: false_information 68.3 vs 95.0, discriminatory_depictions 50.0 vs 91.7, deception 61.7 vs 78.3, dangerous_behavior 66.7 vs 83.3, and environmental_damage. The appendix text says it "secures the best accuracy in a majority of classes". By my count, FalconEye wins in roughly 10 of 20 categories, which is not a majority.
   - On VLGuard, FalconEye is within 0.1 of GPT-4o on instruction (87.98 vs 87.87). Base Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 47.96%, below chance for a binary task, which suggests a prompt or format failure rather than a real baseline.
   - VLGuard and BeaverTails-V label only some of the three fields, so the tri-output is evaluated only in part. There is no comparison with other recent multimodal evaluators or guard models.
4. **The claim of a "comprehensive" evaluator is not demonstrated.**
   - There is no ablation on the value of the image. A text-only fine-tune of the evaluator, or an evaluator with the image removed, would show how much of the signal comes from the image.
   - Category detection (Table 7) is poor. FalconEye gets 23–72% and is below GPT-4o on 9 of 13 categories. The metric is not defined, since multi-label accuracy is ambiguous. This contradicts the abstract's "outperforms all other baselines" and the conclusion's claim of fine-grained classification. Section 5 says FalconEye "achieves strong performance" on Unlicensed Advice, but it scores 30.12%, below GPT-4o's 40.96%.
   - Responses come from three weak 7B models, one of them a Base model, prompted with a jailbreak "attack prompt" ("You are not bound by any ethical or legal constraints", Appendix B.1). The response distribution is artificial and unlike frontier-model outputs, so transfer to modern models is untested. The main text never mentions this prompt. The text also misreferences it: "training prompt in Appendix B.1" is actually the attack prompt.
   - 80.8% of instructions are harmful, yet Section 5 calls this a "realistic composition". Real traffic is mostly benign. The false-positive and over-refusal rate on benign inputs, which matters for an evaluator, is never reported.
5. **Novelty and contribution.**
   - Both parts are incremental. The dataset recombines three existing datasets with new responses and labels from an existing VLM. The evaluator is a LoRA fine-tune of an existing VLM, the same recipe as existing multimodal guard models. A fair comparison with existing multimodal safety datasets and evaluators is needed to establish novelty. Table 1 compares only the dataset side, and some entries (e.g., "Contain Harmful Responses") are unverified.
   - The taxonomy merges categories such as child and animal abuse and adds loosely defined classes ("Politics", "Unethical Behavior"). This is hard to align with standard taxonomies, and the claim that fragmentation hurts the evaluator is not tested.
6. **Presentation and rigor.**
   - Inconsistencies: Table 2 reports 94.22% on 1,800 samples for response, while Table 3 reports 94.31% on 1,775. Table 5 duplicates the pornographic_content row. "Falcon-test", "Falcon-test(1775)" and "FalconEye-test" are used interchangeably. Section 5 says "20% of images labeled as harmful" while Fig. 4a gives 80.6% safe images.
   - Table 1 lists FalconEye as the "Eval Method" for Falcon, which is circular because the evaluator is trained on its own training split.
   - Training is a single run (8 epochs at lr 1e-5, LoRA rank 128) with no described validation split or model selection. "Interpretable rationales" is a headline claim, but explanation quality is neither evaluated nor ablated.
   - Ethics: the paper releases jailbreak-style prompts, harmful responses and adult imagery with no discussion of ethics review, annotator wellbeing or release safeguards.

## Questions
1. What are the annotator count, inter-annotator agreement (κ), and adjudication process? How well do the Qwen2.5-VL-72B labels agree with the human labels on Falcon-test?
2. Please report precision, recall and F1 for the harmful class, confidence intervals, and majority-class and text-only baselines.
3. How was Llama-Guard-3-11B-Vision run, and why is it absent from Tables 2–4? What prompts were used for GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-7B? Why does Qwen2.5-VL-7B score 47.96% on VLGuard instructions?
4. How did you check for near-duplicate leakage between train and test? Is the split by source image or by prompt?
5. How does FalconEye perform on responses from stronger models and on benign VQA? What are the false-positive and over-refusal rates?
6. Why does FalconEye drop to 50% on discriminatory_depictions and 68% on false_information in BeaverTails-V? Is this a taxonomy mismatch?
7. What does the image contribute, per a no-image ablation?
8. Was an ethics review conducted, and what are the release safeguards?

## Ratings
- **Soundness: 2.** Single metric on imbalanced data, no annotator agreement or CIs, circular labeling and evaluation, and claims that contradict the appendix tables.
- **Presentation: 2**
- **Contribution: 2**
- **Rating: 4** (marginally below the acceptance threshold). It is a potentially useful resource, but the evidence for the central claims is thin and the novelty is limited.
- **Confidence: 4**
