# Review: CLAUSE: Agentic Neuro-Symbolic Knowledge Graph Reasoning via Dynamic Learnable Context Engineering

(Text-only review. Figures were not available, so Figs. 2–4 are judged from captions and the surrounding text.)

## Summary
The paper casts context construction for multi-hop KGQA as a constrained MDP. Three cooperating agents act on a question-anchored subgraph. The Subgraph Architect adds or deletes edges, the Path Navigator continues, backtracks or stops a traversal, and the Context Curator selects textualized snippets with a learned STOP. Costs are tracked for edge edits, interaction steps and selected tokens. The agents are trained with "LC-MAPPO", a centralized-critic MAPPO variant. It has a task head and three cost heads, a QMIX-style monotonic mixer, COMA-style counterfactual advantages, and separate Lagrange multipliers updated by dual ascent. The claim is that budgets or prices can be set per query at inference time from a single checkpoint. Experiments cover HotpotQA (distractor), FactKG and MetaQA 1/2/3-hop. CLAUSE reports the best EM@1 everywhere (e.g., 87.3 vs. 78.0 for KG-Agent and 48.0 for GraphRAG on MetaQA-2hop) and the smallest edge budget. Latency is reported relative to Vanilla RAG. A small comparison against MAPPO, fixed-penalty PPO and RCPO and an ablation table are included.

## Strengths
- The problem is well motivated. Treating context assembly as a budgeted sequential decision problem, with step and token costs in the objective, is a sensible deployment-oriented framing. Learned stopping instead of fixed k-hop or top-k is a reasonable idea.
- The three-way factoring (graph editing / path traversal / evidence curation) maps onto three separable costs, and the cost attribution "at source" is clean.
- Separate dual variables per resource fit the setting naturally. The shadow-price interpretation is an appealing way to expose accuracy–latency–cost trade-offs.
- Baselines span pretrained LLMs, RAG, graph-RAG and agent systems with a shared reader (Qwen3-32B). Efficiency is reported alongside accuracy, which is the right thing to do for this claim. The abstract's headline numbers are arithmetically consistent with the tables.
- The auditable traces and the case study illustrate the intended provenance benefit.

## Weaknesses

1. **The central claim (per-query budgets/prices adjustable at test time without retraining) is not demonstrated.** The Introduction mentions "requirement sweeps" that "reveal clear accuracy–latency–cost Pareto frontiers", and the conclusion says the method "traces stronger accuracy–efficiency frontiers". No budget or price sweep appears anywhere in the paper. There is no Pareto plot, and no result varies β or λ at inference. All main tables are a single operating point. There is no evidence that a model trained under one budget respects a different budget at test time. It is also unclear how the policy is conditioned on β/λ (is b_t an input, and are budgets sampled during training?). This is the headline novelty and it is unsupported.

2. **Latency and "predictable runtime" claims are weak or contradicted by the results.** The abstract says CLAUSE reduces "end-to-end latency". Table 2 shows it is slower than Vanilla RAG in 4 of 5 settings (1.48, 1.36, 1.14, 1.27×) and slower than LightRAG everywhere; on FactKG it is nearly equal to GraphRAG (1.36 vs. 1.35). The latency wins are against agent baselines and, on MetaQA-2hop, GraphRAG. "Predictable" runtime is never measured (no variance or tail latency, no budget-violation rate for the main system). Latency is a normalized ratio, with no absolute times, hardware, or description of how the RL controller's own overhead is counted. The only absolute number is 238.6 ms in the case study. Latency is defined as interaction steps in the method but reported as wall-clock ratios; the two are conflated.

3. **The constraint-satisfaction experiment (Fig. 3) is unconvincing and inconsistent with the main results.** It uses an edge budget of 0.5 and a latency budget of 0.7, yet CLAUSE in the main tables has edge 0.74–0.90 and latency ≥0.98, so the budgets evaluated are mostly infeasible for the method itself. The best feasibility rate is 0.340 (vs. 0.117 for MAPPO), meaning LC-MAPPO violates constraints about two-thirds of the time. A "191% improvement" over a very low baseline is not a sign of reliable constraint enforcement. The latency violation rate remains 0.577. The "constraint learning" panel treats a larger dual variable value (0.004 vs. 0.001) as "stronger learning", which is not a meaningful quality metric. There are no seeds, error bars, or task-accuracy numbers for these runs. The text does not define violation or feasibility. Only MetaQA is studied.

4. **Missing training and implementation details; reproducibility.** The paper does not say how the agents are trained: the definition of Racc, the environment/trajectory setup, the number of episodes, which data is used for RL vs. evaluation, and the hyperparameters (η, λ initialisation, H, scorer architectures, weights w1–w4 and whether they are learned). It does not say whether the reader LLM is in the training loop. The algorithms, the convergence statement and the shadow-price proof are all deferred to "Appendix ??", which is unresolved in the submission, so these claims cannot be evaluated. Convergence of dual ascent with PPO and function approximation is non-trivial. The shadow-price identity holds only at an exact optimum of a (convex) constrained problem, which is not the case here, so the claim is stronger than warranted.

5. **Method description is internally unclear.** (a) Mixing QMIX-style monotonic mixing with COMA counterfactual advantages and a MAPPO surrogate is described in two sentences, and the monotonic-mixer restriction that Related Work criticises is then adopted. (b) The Architect's gain–price rule is a deterministic argmax over a hand-fused score s(e|q,Gt) − λ·c, which looks like a heuristic rather than a learned policy; how is it trained with PPO, and how are w1–w4 learned? (c) The episode "ends when all modules stop or a budget is exhausted", which implies the caps are enforced by truncation. That is a different mechanism from the expected-cost constraints in Eq. 1, and "hard guarantees" (Sec. 4.5) are not given by an expectation constraint. (d) How edits feed traversal and curation, and what the reader sees, is only sketched. (e) Clat counts interaction steps, but Table 2 measures wall-clock.

6. **The experimental setup is under-specified and possibly non-comparable.** HotpotQA is a text-passage dataset; how a KG is built for it, and what "edges" mean there, is not stated. FactKG is a claim-verification task, and EM@1 for it is unexplained. MetaQA numbers for baselines look odd (e.g. Vanilla RAG 33.0 on 3-hop, while 1-hop is typically near saturation for classical KGQA systems). CLAUSE's 91.0 on MetaQA-1hop is itself below what specialised methods are known to report on that dataset (~97–100). The paper does not compare with specialised KGQA systems or recent LLM+KG reasoning methods (Think-on-Graph, RoG, SubgraphRAG, GNN-RAG), nor evaluate on standard KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP, CWQ). Many baselines (LightRAG, GraphRAG, AutoGen, GoT) are general-purpose and seem to be applied with minimal tuning. It is not stated how baselines were configured, or whether they were trained or tuned on the same data as CLAUSE, which is RL-trained while most baselines are zero-shot. Without this, much of the gap (+39 EM over GraphRAG, +9–10 over KG-Agent on 2/3-hop) may reflect in-distribution training rather than the budgeted-context idea. A missing control is a supervised or fine-tuned retriever/reader baseline using the same training data.

7. **No statistical reporting.** All results are single numbers without seeds, confidence intervals or significance tests. Fig. 2 is labelled "mean ± SD", but the underlying runs are unspecified. RL training is high-variance, and several ablation gaps (85.0 vs. 84.6 vs. 87.3) are small.

8. **The token-budget claim rests only on a figure.** The abstract claims "equal or lower token budgets", but the evidence is a bar plot of normalised token mass (Fig. 2) with family averages, not per-method numbers. Token budgets are not controlled across methods. A fair comparison would be accuracy at matched token budgets, which the Introduction claims ("at the matched token mass") but the paper does not show.

9. **Ablation issues.** Table 4 is on "MetaQA" (87.3 matches the 2-hop result, but this is not stated) and uses a different normalisation (CLAUSE = 1.0×) from the main tables. The "w/o Architect" variant is "StaticRAG; no-KG", which removes the KG entirely, not just the editing agent, so it does not isolate the Architect's contribution. "Greedy-Hop" and "Top-k Rerank" are loosely defined. There are no ablations of the multi-head cost critic, the monotonic mixer, COMA advantages, edit reversibility or PID stabilisation, which are the claimed algorithmic contributions. The LC-MAPPO vs. RCPO comparison appears only in Fig. 3, without EM.

10. **Provenance and neuro-symbolic claims are not evaluated.** There is no measurement of provenance quality (path faithfulness, supporting-fact recall, human audit). The case study is a single trivial 2-hop example (36 tokens of context against a 512-token budget), which says little about budget-aware behaviour.

11. **Presentation.** Unresolved "Appendix ??" references, the odd phrase "without relying on multi-agent expansion" in the token paragraph, undefined terminology ("requirements", "dials", "prices"), and over-interpretation in the results text (e.g. "validate LC-MAPPO's design") weaken the paper. "Agentic" here means small policies in a fixed three-stage loop rather than LLM agents; this should be stated clearly.

## Questions
1. Please show results for varying β/λ at inference time from a single checkpoint (EM vs. latency/tokens/edges, with violation rates). Is the policy conditioned on budgets during training, and over what range?
2. Why does CLAUSE exceed Vanilla RAG latency in most settings while the abstract states a latency reduction? What is the latency breakdown (controller, retrieval, reader), and what hardware and absolute times apply?
3. How were baselines trained or tuned? Do CLAUSE and the baselines have access to the same training data? Please add a supervised fine-tuned baseline using the same data and a non-agentic learned retriever.
4. How is a KG constructed for HotpotQA, and what is EM@1 for FactKG? Why are specialised MetaQA methods and WebQSP/CWQ results absent?
5. What is the reward Racc, how is the reader included in training, and what are the training set sizes, compute, hyperparameters, number of seeds and variance?
6. Why is the best constraint feasibility only 0.34, and why were budgets of 0.5/0.7 (infeasible for the main configuration) chosen? Please report accuracy alongside violation rates.
7. How is the Architect's argmax rule trained by PPO, and are w1–w4 learned? Do the monotonic mixer and COMA advantage coexist consistently in the critic?
8. Please provide the appendix (algorithms, convergence, shadow-price derivation) and state the assumptions under which the shadow-price property holds.

## Scores
- **Soundness:** 2 / 4
- **Presentation:** 2 / 4
- **Contribution:** 2 / 4

## Rating: 4 (marginally below the acceptance threshold)
The idea of a budget-aware, learned controller for KG context construction is reasonable and the reported gains are large. However, the headline capability (test-time budget/price control) is not demonstrated, the constraint-satisfaction evidence is weak, baseline fairness and training details are unclear, and the algorithmic content is largely a combination of existing components (MAPPO + QMIX mixer + COMA + Lagrangian duals) with the key proofs and algorithms missing from the submission. There is no variance reporting.

## Confidence: 4
